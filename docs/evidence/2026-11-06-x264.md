# Evidence: SPEC CPU2017 525.x264_r against native, and the lowering fixes it found (2026-11-06)

Host: AMD Ryzen AI MAX+ 395 (Zen 5), Linux 6.12.113, .NET 10.0.14 (ILCompiler 10.0.14), Debian clang 22.1.8; llvmnet links the same `libLLVM.so.22.1`. Base commit `adfdc44`. Linux ARM64: the arm64 .NET container under qemu. A development measurement of one SPEC workload, not a SPEC score; the scripts are in the gitignored `artifacts/x264-perf/` (`build.sh`, `run.py`, `step.sh`, `tools/`), and the SPEC sources stay in the licensed kit.

## The workload and the builds

The first refrate invocation of 525.x264_r, `--pass 1 --stats x264_stats.log --bitrate 1000 --frames 1000 -o BuckBunny_New.264 BuckBunny.yuv 1280x720`: x264's fast first pass (diamond search, subme 2, one reference, no 8x8 transform or inter sub-partitions) with the default lookahead. The input is decoded from `BuckBunny.264` by SPEC's `ldecod_r`. The 36 sources are compiled with SPEC's flags (`-DSPEC -DNDEBUG -DSPEC_AUTO_SUPPRESS_OPENMP -DSPEC_AUTO_BYTEORDER=0x12345678 -DSPEC_LP64 -fcommon -O3`; portable mode adds `-include string.h -include stdlib.h`, as `config/spec/llvmnet.cfg` does), natively with `clang-22` and glibc, without LTO, and with a packaged llvmnet toolchain in portable mode, linked `--nativeaot`. llvmnet's link only internalizes and removes dead globals, so neither side inlines across files.

| Pair | Native | llvmnet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| scalar | `-O3 -fno-vectorize -fno-slp-vectorize` | the same; NativeAOT baseline (no vector code) |
| SSE2 / Vector128 | `-O3` (x86-64 baseline) | `-O3`; NativeAOT `x86-64-v2` for vector code (the default) |
| x86-64-v3 | `-O3 -march=x86-64-v3` | `-O3 --isa=x86-64-v3` (256-bit fragments, NativeAOT `x86-64-v3`) |

Each time is the median of three runs, one process at a time, pinned to one core and its SMT sibling (`taskset -c 2,18`), every run from a fresh copy of the executable (below). Every build of every configuration writes the same `BuckBunny_New.264` (md5 `225bcd8b…`) and `x264_stats.log` (md5 `15c045d5…`). The vectorizers make the same decisions for `x86_64-unknown-none` and `x86_64-pc-linux-gnu` at each level (compared on `mc.c` and `dct.c`), so every gap is in the lowering and in ILC.

## SV-24: per-lane vector shifts

At x86-64-v3 `sub4x4_dct` ran 13.8 times native, 35% of the gap. Its SLP-vectorized IR shifts in two ways:

- `shl <8 x i16> %72, <i16 poison, i16 poison, i16 poison, i16 poison, i16 1, i16 1, i16 1, i16 1>` is a shift by 1. `SplatScalar` wanted every constant lane to be a `ConstantInt` and every mask entry of a splat shuffle defined, so the shift went to `Vectors.ShiftLeft<T>`, a `WithElement` loop: inlined, an eight-iteration loop that stored the vector, wrote two bytes of it and loaded the 16 bytes back on every lane, a store-forwarding failure each, chained through the vector.
- `shl nsw <4 x i32> %34, %35` has counts per lane (two lanes come from data and are discarded later). It called `Vectors.ShiftLeft<Int32>` out of line: four `pextrd`/`shlx`/`pinsrd` rounds through a return buffer, where AVX2 has `vpsllvd`.

What changed:

- `SplatScalar` ignores poison and undef lanes of a constant and undefined entries of a splat shuffle's mask.
- A shift left by a constant count per lane in 16- or 32-bit lanes is a multiplication by the powers of two, as LLVM's x86 backend computes it (`pmullw`, `pmulld`), one instruction on every host.
- `Vectors.ShiftLeft`, `ShiftRightLogical` and `ShiftRightArithmetic` (128 and 256 bits) run AVX2's `vpsllvd`/`q`, `vpsrlvd`/`q` and `vpsravd`, 16-bit lanes widened to 32 bits, and AdvSimd's `ushl` and `sshl` for every width (a negative count shifts right); the lane loop is out of line and goes through one buffer, for SSE-only x64 and software. Counts are taken modulo the lane width on every path, as the loop always did (a count at or above the width is poison in LLVM).

`sub4x4_dct` at x86-64-v3 is 199 instructions instead of 269, with no calls and 7 vector spills instead of 47, and takes 0.68 s instead of 3.85.

## SV-25: vector getelementptr as address arithmetic

A function with a vector `getelementptr` was scalarized whole, because the lowering has no GEP of pointer vectors. The loop vectorizer makes them in its runtime alias checks: x264's `frame_init_lowres_core` has one, `getelementptr i8, <2 x ptr> %40, i64 1` in a loop preheader, and at x86-64-v3 the function ran 26 times native as scalar code (22,771 bytes of machine code against native's 2,095); `x264_macroblock_analyse` was scalarized at both levels for the same reason.

What changed: the driver expands vector GEPs before the lowering (`llvmnet_expand_vector_geps`, [native/vectors.cpp](../../native/vectors.cpp)), as `ptrtoint` of the base (a scalar base splatted), plus each index sign-extended to 64 bits and scaled by its element size (a shift for a power of two, a multiplication otherwise), plus struct field offsets and constant indices folded into one addend, and `inttoptr`; the lowering translates all of those.

**Correctness.** [tests/ir/vector-lowering.ll](../../tests/ir/vector-lowering.ll)'s `pointers` adds a vector base with a scalar index, a scalar base with vector indices, a struct field between vector indices and a variable vector index with a stride of 6; it passes natively and in both modes, and [tests/vector-lowering.sh](../../tests/vector-lowering.sh) checks that the typed build keeps `pointers` as vector code. The vector tests, `semantics` and `ir` pass.

## SV-26: psadbw's sums without the zero folds

x264's 8-wide SADs reduce every row on its own (`llvm.vector.reduce.add` of one `zext(abs(sub))` leaf), which SV-21 makes a `psadbw` of the row padded to 16 bytes. The reduction then summed the result's four 32-bit lanes, two shifts and two adds, although psadbw leaves lanes 1 and 3 zero and the padding's sum is zero; the padding itself was an `and` per operand, although the 8-byte load (`movq`) had zeroed those lanes; and at 256 bits every load of at most 16 bytes cleared the upper half with `vxorps` and `vinserti128`. `x264_pixel_sad_x4_8x8` was 522 instructions at Vector128 against native's 187, and ran 1.7 to 2.0 times native.

What changed:

- A reduction of one SAD leaf adds psadbw's results as 64-bit lanes and extracts only the lanes that summed bytes ([native/vectors.cpp](../../native/vectors.cpp), `Replace`): one `movq` for an 8-byte row, `pextrq` and an add for 16 bytes.
- A shuffle that pads a vector load of fewer bytes than a fragment with zeros, keeping its lanes in place, emits no `and` (`VectorEmitter.Gather`): `Vectors.Load` reads the bytes into the low lanes and zeros above.
- `Vectors.LoadWide` widens a load of at most 16 bytes with `ToVector256`, which leaves no instruction after a VEX load, instead of `Vector256.Create` with a zero half.

`x264_pixel_sad_x4_8x8` is 265 instructions at Vector128 (`psadbw` 32, `movq` 96, the rest addresses and the frame) and 362 at x86-64-v3 instead of 557.

## PF-21: sign extension of 8- and 16-bit integers

`ValueEmitter.SignExtend` sign-extended every width by a shift pair (`shl`, `shr` by 32 minus the width). For an `int16_t` load that became `movzx`, `shl 16`, `sar 16`, where native code and RyuJIT's own C# code have one `movsx r, WORD PTR [...]`: RyuJIT folds `conv.i2` (and `conv.i1`) into the load before it, but not a shift pair. x264 keeps coefficients, motion vectors and filter intermediates in `int16_t`; `hpel_filter` ran 1.34 times native in the scalar build, the largest scalar gap.

What changed: widths 8 and 16 sign-extend with `conv.i1` and `conv.i2`; other widths keep the shift pair. `hpel_filter` is 198 instructions instead of 210 (native 154), with 6 `movsx` and no `shl`.

## SV-27: constant vector shift counts as immediates

A shift of vector lanes by one count stored the count in a local before the fragments' shifts. RyuJIT chooses between the immediate form (`pslld xmm, 16`) and the form with the count in a register when it imports the call, before it propagates the local's constant, so every constant shift took its count from memory (`movss xmm2, [constant]`, `pslld xmm1, xmm2`): x264's `satd_8x4` shifts by 16 four times per call that way.

What changed: a constant count (a splat of a constant, or the SLP vectorizer's partial splat since SV-24) is pushed at each fragment's shift, as are the two counts of a funnel shift by a constant (`VectorEmitter.Binary`, `FunnelShift`).

The NativeAOT images' managed code has 248 vector shifts by an immediate and 51 by a register or memory count at Vector128, against 31 and 268 before, and 358 and 31 at x86-64-v3, against 63 and 326.

## SV-28: rounding averages as pavgb and pavgw

C's `(a + b + 1) >> 1` on bytes is, after the vectorizers, `lshr(add(add(zext a, zext b), 1), 1)` in 16-bit lanes (or 32-bit for 16-bit values), which LLVM's x86 backend matches to `pavgb` and `pavgw` (`detectAVGPattern`). llvmnet computed the widened arithmetic: x264's `frame_init_lowres_core` (an average of two averages per output byte, 12 of them per loop body at Vector128), `get_ref` and `mc_luma` (half-pel averages of two predictions) have it, as do the half-pel interpolations of every video codec.

What changed: `llvmnet_rounding_averages` ([native/vectors.cpp](../../native/vectors.cpp)) runs with SV-21's recognizer on the functions that stay vector code. A logical shift right by one of a sum of two values and the constant 1, the adds in any order, whose two values have no bits above 8 (or 16) bits by LLVM's known bits, becomes `llvm.x86.sse2.pavg.b` or `pavg.w` on 16-byte pieces (AVX2's forms on 32 bytes with 256-bit fragments), whose helpers run `Sse2.Average`, AdvSimd's `urhadd` or an exact composition; a `trunc` of the shift to the narrow lanes is the average itself, and an average of averages takes the inner results without widening them. `-v` and `--report-simd` ("averages of") name the functions.

**Correctness.** [tests/c/vector-reductions.c](../../tests/c/vector-reductions.c) adds averages of bytes and of 16-bit values over every epilogue length, x264's nested lowres filter, and look-alikes that must keep their literal code: the average without rounding, of signed bytes, and with a rounding constant of 2. The vector tests, `x86-intrinsics`, `simd128`, `semantics`, `ir`, `driver-options` and `driver-reference` pass.
